Welcome. I see therapy as a dynamic process that can help people feel freer and more engaged in the world as they would like to be. I have a special interest in working with students, people in creative fields, LGBTQIA+ teens and adults, children of immigrants, and people whose political commitments are an important part of their lives. My work often includes depression, anxiety, trauma, and attachment wounds, and I pay close attention to the larger social and cultural forces that shape how people experience themselves and their relationships. I welcome clients from all backgrounds and worldviews, including those that differ from my own.

My style is warm and direct. I seek to build a relationship where you can think out loud, change your mind, and say things before they are fully formed. Clients have described me as steady and grounded, especially when conversations feel difficult or emotionally charged.

I respect your decision to seek support, knowing it is not always an easy one. When you’re ready, please email, call, or schedule a free 15-minute consultation. I look forward to meeting you.

About

Before starting my private practice, I worked in community mental health providing counseling services to clients of all ages and from all walks of life. I received my bachelor’s and master’s degrees from the University of Texas at Austin. I have lived in Austin for over a decade and have developed a deep appreciation for the city and the people who live here.

My approach is shaped by my interests in ecology, philosophy, literature, music, meditation, and movement, as well as by a longstanding interest in community, relationships, and the ways people make meaning of their lives.

Services

Individual Therapy

I work with teens and adults around a range of concerns, including:

  • Anxiety, perfectionism, shame, self-doubt, and fear of failure or success
  • Depression, grief, trauma, and difficulty understanding or expressing emotions
  • Relationship patterns that keep repeating, including difficulty with closeness, conflict, authority, work, or institutions
  • Questions of identity and belonging, including cultural and spiritual identity, sexuality, gender, and navigating life between cultures
  • Neurodivergence, masking, and the strain of trying to meet expectations that do not fit
  • Teens navigating school pressure, friendships, identity, family expectations, and growing independence

You do not need to have a diagnosis or a clear explanation for what is wrong to begin therapy. Sometimes people come in because they know something is not working the way they want it to, even if they are not yet sure how to make sense of it.

Some concerns benefit from more specialized or intensive care than I offer in my practice. I do not currently treat active eating disorders, active substance dependence, or primary psychotic disorders. If you are looking for help with one of these concerns, I am happy to help point you toward appropriate resources.

Relationship Therapy

I work with couples, families, and other important relationships when people feel caught with one another or want to understand the relationship differently. This can include monogamous and polyamorous relationships, chosen family, co-parents, friends, and small groups who share an important relationship or common purpose.

Group Therapy (Coming Soon)

Pricing

Pricing Philosophy

I see the financial arrangement as part of the therapeutic work. I value being able to talk openly about money and whatever feelings come up around it. I try to set fees that are manageable enough for therapy to continue over time, while still reflecting the value and commitment of the work.

A Note About Insurance

I have chosen to work outside of insurance networks so that decisions about therapy can stay between us as much as possible. Insurance generally requires a mental health diagnosis and can involve sharing clinical information to justify or continue treatment.

Private pay gives us more freedom to decide together what kind of work makes sense, how often to meet, and how long therapy should continue. It also leaves more room for people whose reasons for coming to therapy may not fit neatly into a diagnosis.

I know private pay is not accessible to everyone, which is part of why I offer a range of fees and can provide superbills for clients with out-of-network benefits.

Choosing a Therapist

Research consistently finds that the quality of the therapeutic relationship is one of the strongest predictors of a positive outcome in therapy (Flückiger et al., 2018).

For that reason, I encourage you to consider more than cost or insurance status when choosing a therapist. If you are able, talk with more than one person and pay attention to whether you can imagine being honest with them, asking questions, disagreeing, and working through difficult moments together.

Your Right to a Good Faith Estimate

You have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of the expected cost of your care. You may request an estimate at any time.

If a bill is at least $400 more than your Good Faith Estimate, you may have the right to dispute it.
